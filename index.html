01 / THE QUESTION

Well distributed.
By what measure?

“Public bicycles are well distributed across the areas where they are most needed.”

Our research puts that familiar assumption to the test. Observed rentals reveal completed trips. When a station is empty, they cannot reveal everyone who wanted to ride.

H₀Supply is proportional to effective demand across areas.
H₁At least one area’s supply share differs from its effective demand share.

Demand definition: The hypothesis concerns effective demand, including unmet requests. The displayed allocation and regression use predicted daily demand: 4,312 allocated rows use historical normal-day averages and 630 use regression fallback. These are proxies, not validated effective demand.

V2 adds nominal dock counts, midnight inventory, and district labels. We reanalyze the capacity-demand association and inspect a fixed-fleet redistribution scenario. Neither a significant slope nor a reallocation plan alone settles the original regional effective-demand H₀.

Proxy-level proportionality result: The nonredundant HC3 Wald test rejects the zero-intercept restriction in the station-level predicted-demand model (Weekday: χ²(1) = 1,731.69; Weekend: χ²(1) = 3,463.30; p < 10−300). Thus, strict proportionality is rejected within this predicted-demand proxy model. This does not constitute a formal rejection of the original regional effective-demand H₀.

EXPLORATORY REGRESSION / HC3

Does capacity follow demand?

Docksᵢ = β₀ + β₁ × (Dᵢ / 100)

D is the supplied mean predicted daily demand, not independently validated effective demand. A slope p-value tests β₁ = 0, not proportional allocation.

PROPORTIONALITY / BENCHMARK

Compare the whole line.

A proportional line has zero intercept and slope k = total nominal docks / total predicted demand. The requested two-condition Wald holds this observed k fixed, so its χ²(2) p-value is conditional and exploratory.

MASTER + EXPORT AUDIT

Conservation is explicit.

The integer movement balance, soft bounds, exclusions and every district subtotal match the supplied exports. The 80,601-row master reproduces every target and rating. Integer starting-stock ties produce one-bike differences in 222 rows; displayed results retain the team exports.

EXPLORATORY PROPORTIONALITY CHECK

Test the restrictions, state the reference.

Joint restrictions: β₀ = 0 and β₁ = k, in docks / 100 requests. For OLS with an intercept, k = ȳ/x̄ = β̂₁ + β̂₀/x̄. Because k is calculated from these same observations, the second condition adds no independent restriction when β₀ = 0. The nonredundant HC3 Wald uses χ²(1). A χ²(2) comparison that treats k as fixed is also reported as a conditional sensitivity check. Neither is the planned regional effective-demand test or causal evidence.

HC3 intervals address heteroskedasticity. Spatial dependence, generated-demand uncertainty, selection into the allocation pool and rounding are not propagated. These are exploratory associations.

Slope is docks per 100 additional predicted requests/day. Intercept is docks at zero predicted demand; it is a model parameter, not a literal zero-demand station forecast.

WHY HIDDEN FEATURES MATTER

A non-significant variable
is not an irrelevant variable.

Limited evidence. Measurement error, collinearity, insufficient variation and nonlinear effects can hide a meaningful relationship. No coefficients were estimated for these hidden features. Their statistical significance remains unknown.

Different policy stakes. A budget or sidewalk constraint may explain a shortage even without a measured coefficient. Ignoring it could send resources where installation is easier, rather than where access is needed.

A careful next step. Verify dock counts and midnight inventory definitions; obtain weather and rebalancing records. Test conditional relationships and practical effect sizes before changing operations.

Data provenance & method notes

Authoritative analytical sources: the final master, revised recommendations, district summary and system check CSVs; build_final_v2.py; README_최종분석_v2.md. The 5,389 rows represent station × weekday/weekend summaries, not daily observations. V2 replaces the earlier dataset's analytical figures on this page.

Export audit: station keys, integer targets, target − current movements, total conservation, bounds, exclusions, action counts and district subtotals were checked. The supplied script was rerun on the 80,601-row master (2,695 stations, 1–30 September, no duplicate station-date keys). All target quantities and ratings match. Current-inventory largest-remainder ties differ by one bike in 222 rows, changing 64 action labels; both assignments satisfy the rounding rule. The team exports are retained for display. Rerun movement totals are 4,876 / 4,212 versus supplied 4,874 / 4,206; 12 need values differ by 0.1 from numerical rounding. Demand-model training and held-out evaluation remain unverified.

Wald interpretation: The joint fixed-k χ²(2) result is conditional. Since k is estimated from this sample, OLS implies β̂₁ − k = −β̂₀/x̄; the independent proportionality restriction is the zero intercept, tested with χ²(1). The original regional effective-demand hypothesis is not tested here.

Regression: separate station-level fits for Weekday (n=2,472) and Weekend (n=2,470), using the ALLOCATED subset. Response = nominal docks; predictor = mean supplied predicted daily demand / 100. OLS with HC3 covariance and t-based 95% intervals. This is exploratory reanalysis of rounded aggregates, not the original stratified-sample result or a causal test.

Model confidence: HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW are supplied rule-based recommendation ratings. STRONG requires all five allocation variants to agree in direction at a magnitude of at least two bikes, using upstream continuous values. Rounded exported sensitivity values can obscure threshold cases.

Stockouts and inventory: stockout_rate_mean is a supplied mean daily snapshot ratio. It is not a measured continuous empty duration. B is mean midnight stock. Daily rebalancing is not observed. Recommendation-stockout correlation is partly mechanical and does not independently validate the model.

Normal-day definition: A normal day is a station-day with no hourly snapshot showing zero available bicycles. Predicted demand first uses the station's mean observed rentals on normal days of the same weekpart when at least 3 such days are available. If fewer than 3 exist, the station's mean across all normal days is used when at least 5 are available. Otherwise, a regression fallback is used. In the final allocation pool, 4,312 station-weekpart rows use historical-baseline demand and 630 use regression fallback.

Capacity and exclusions: effective upper bounds are assumed soft limits; 1,889 targets exceed nominal docks. Inactive, depot/repair and extreme inventory-to-dock ratios are excluded from the allocation pool. There are 242 missing normal-day counts within allocated rows. No verified operational capacity or feasibility claim is made.

Public data scope: Downloads contain station-day aggregates, public station names/locations and derived demand/supply fields, not rider or trip-level identifiers. The team is responsible for confirming the original datasets’ redistribution terms.

Geography: coordinates come from the supplied final master by station ID. Analytical displays retain the supplied v2 exports.

Methods documentation: statsmodels OLS · HC3 robust covariance. Full methods and limitations are included in the reports and source package.
